I sat SC-900 today and passed. A few weeks ago I wrote about studying it while job hunting — Entra ID, MFA, and Conditional Access showing up in every second ad, and me not being able to explain any of them properly if someone actually asked. That post was about *why* I started. This one is about what survived the exam, now that I have to say I finished it.

The exam was less about memorizing product names and more about knowing *what problem each piece of the Microsoft security stack is for*. Identity and access still took up most of the room in my head: who someone is, how you prove it, and what they're allowed to touch. Conditional Access made more sense as a set of rules than as a buzzword — "if this user, from this place, on this device, then require this." Defender and Purview showed up as the other two legs of the stool: threats and protection on one side, data and compliance on the other. Once I stopped treating them as a list of logos and started treating them as jobs, the questions got easier.

What helped most wasn't a dump of practice questions. It was having an Active Directory lab running at the same time. Creating users, groups, and a domain controller by hand made Entra ID feel like a continuation of something I already understood, not a cloud product I'd only seen in screenshots. SC-900 didn't ask me to configure any of that in a portal. It did ask me to know *why* those controls exist, and the lab had already forced me to care about that.

I want to be honest about what this cert is. SC-900 is a fundamentals exam. It does not make me a security engineer, and it does not replace hands-on work in a real tenant. What it does give me is language I was missing: I can talk about identity, Conditional Access, Defender, and Purview without hoping nobody follows up. For someone coming out of support work and into infrastructure, that gap was the whole point of sitting it.

Next is the same plan as before, just with one box actually ticked. Keep the lab growing. Keep going on CCNA. Keep working through Security+. And keep pairing whatever I'm studying with something concrete I'm trying to get — a job, a lab, a skill I can demonstrate — rather than collecting badges for their own sake.
